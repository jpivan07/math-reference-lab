1. Quadratic Formula

x = −b ± √b2 − 4ac 2a

Finds the exact roots (solutions) of any standard quadratic equation ax2 + bx + c = 0 where a ≠ 0.

a: quadratic coeff. b: linear coeff. c: constant term
Solve: 2x2 − 5x − 3 = 0
  1. Step 1: Identify Coefficients a = 2,   b = −5,   c = −3
  2. Step 2: Compute Discriminant (Δ = b² − 4ac) Δ = (−5)2 − 4(2)(−3) = 25 + 24 = 49
  3. Step 3: Substitute into Formula x = (5 ± √49) / 4 = (5 ± 7) / 4
Roots: x1 = 3,   x2 = −0.5

2. Two-Point Slope & Line Equation

m = y2 − y1 x2 − x1  •  y = mx + b

Determines the constant rate of change (slope m) between two Cartesian points and constructs the slope-intercept line equation.

(x1, y1), (x2, y2): points m: slope b: y-intercept
Find the line passing through (2, 3) and (6, 11).
  1. Step 1: Compute Slope (m) m = (11 − 3) / (6 − 2) = 8 / 4 = 2
  2. Step 2: Solve for y-Intercept (b = y₁ − mx₁) b = 3 − 2(2) = 3 − 4 = −1
Equation: y = 2x − 1  (m = 2, b = −1)

3. Compound Interest Formula

A = P(1 + r n )nt

Calculates the accumulated future value A of an initial principal P when interest compounds n times per year over t years.

P: principal r: annual rate (%) n: compounds/yr t: years
Invest P = 10,000 at 6% annual interest compounded monthly (n = 12) for 5 years.
  1. Step 1: Convert Rate & Periodic Factor r = 0.06,   1 + r/n = 1 + 0.06/12 = 1.005
  2. Step 2: Total Compounding Periods (nt) nt = 12 × 5 = 60 periods
  3. Step 3: Evaluate Power & Multiply A = 10,000 × (1.005)60 ≈ 10,000 × 1.34885
Final Amount: A = 13,488.50  (Interest: 3,488.50)

4. Pythagorean Theorem

a2 + b2 = c2  ⇒  c = √a2 + b2

Relates the two perpendicular legs (a, b) of a right-angled triangle to the length of its longest side, the hypotenuse (c).

a, b: perpendicular legs c: hypotenuse
Find hypotenuse c for a right triangle with legs a = 5 and b = 12.
  1. Step 1: Square Both Legs a2 + b2 = 52 + 122 = 25 + 144 = 169
  2. Step 2: Take the Principal Square Root c = √169 = 13
Hypotenuse: c = 13 units

5. Circle Area & Circumference

A = πr2  •  C = 2πr

Computes the enclosed interior area A and outer boundary perimeter (circumference C) of a circle from its radius r.

r: radius π ≈ 3.141593
Calculate area and circumference for a circle of radius r = 7 cm.
  1. Step 1: Compute Area (A = πr²) A = π(7)2 = 49π ≈ 153.9380 cm2
  2. Step 2: Compute Circumference (C = 2πr) C = 2π(7) = 14π ≈ 43.9823 cm
A ≈ 153.94 cm2,   C ≈ 43.98 cm

6. Cylinder Volume & Surface Area

V = πr2h  •  SA = 2πrh + 2πr2

Measures the 3D interior capacity (V) and total exterior surface area (SA, lateral wall plus two circular caps) of a right cylinder.

r: base radius h: vertical height
Find volume and surface area for r = 3 m and h = 10 m.
  1. Step 1: Compute Volume (V = πr²h) V = π(32)(10) = 90π ≈ 282.7433 m3
  2. Step 2: Lateral + Base Areas (SA) SA = 2π(3)(10) + 2π(32) = 60π + 18π = 78π ≈ 245.0442 m2

7. Law of Cosines

c2 = a2 + b2 − 2ab cos(C)

Generalizes the Pythagorean theorem to any triangle, solving for the third side c given two sides (a, b) and their included angle C.

a, b: known sides C: included angle (deg)
Given a = 8, b = 11, and included angle C = 60°, find side c.
  1. Step 1: Evaluate cos(60°) cos(60°) = 0.5
  2. Step 2: Substitute into c² c2 = 82 + 112 − 2(8)(11)(0.5) = 64 + 121 − 88 = 97
  3. Step 3: Take Square Root c = √97 ≈ 9.8489
Side Length: c ≈ 9.8489 units

8. Right Triangle SOH-CAH-TOA

sin(θ) = OppHyp   cos(θ) = AdjHyp   tan(θ) = OppAdj

Defines primary trigonometric ratios of acute angle θ from the opposite and adjacent legs of a right triangle.

Opp: opposite leg Adj: adjacent leg θ = arctan(Opp/Adj)
Given Opposite = 3 and Adjacent = 4, find Hypotenuse, θ, and trig ratios.
  1. Step 1: Compute Hypotenuse Hyp = √(32 + 42) = √25 = 5
  2. Step 2: Solve for Angle θ θ = arctan(3 / 4) ≈ 36.8699° (0.6435 rad)
  3. Step 3: Evaluate Ratios sin(θ) = 0.6,   cos(θ) = 0.8,   tan(θ) = 0.75

r: radius θrad = θdeg × (π/180)

10. Power Rule Derivative

ddx[a xn] = a n xn−1

Finds the instantaneous rate of change (derivative function f′(x)) and evaluates the exact tangent slope at a point x0.

a: coefficient n: exponent x0: evaluation point
Differentiate f(x) = 4x3 and evaluate at x0 = 2.
  1. Step 1: Apply Power Rule f′(x) = 4 · 3 · x3−1 = 12x2
  2. Step 2: Evaluate Tangent Slope at x₀ = 2 f′(2) = 12(2)2 = 12 × 4 = 48
f′(x) = 12x2  •  Slope f′(2) = 48

c: coeff. n: power (≠ −1) a, b: lower/upper limits
Evaluate ∫13 3x2 dx.
  1. Step 1: Find Antiderivative F(x) F(x) = 3x2+1 / (2 + 1) = x3
  2. Step 2: Evaluate F(3) − F(1) F(3) − F(1) = 33 − 13 = 27 − 1 = 26

12. Average Rate of Change (f(x) = x2)

AROC = f(b) − f(a) b − a = b2 − a2 b − a

Measures the slope of the secant line connecting (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b)) on f(x) = x2 over the closed interval [a, b].

a: interval start b: interval end (b ≠ a)
Find the average rate of change of f(x) = x2 on [1, 4].
  1. Step 1: Evaluate Function Endpoints f(1) = 12 = 1,   f(4) = 42 = 16
  2. Step 2: Compute Difference Quotient AROC = (16 − 1) / (4 − 1) = 15 / 3 = 5
Average Rate of Change (Secant Slope): 5
